MY STORY

One Teacher.
One Mission.

My name is Catherine Lukale, and I am a Special Education teacher in Kenya.

My story is one of hope, resilience, and the life-changing impact of kindness. I was raised in a humble background where pursuing an education often seemed beyond my reach. Someone chose to believe in me, held my hand, and gave me the opportunity to continue my education. That single act of generosity changed the course of my life forever.

Because someone invested in my future, I chose to invest my life in the futures of others. My passion led me into Special Needs Education, where I have the privilege of supporting children with diverse abilities, helping them learn, grow, and discover the confidence to reach their full potential.

Beyond the classroom, my heart is devoted to orphaned and vulnerable children. I strive to be the helping hand that I once needed, offering encouragement, care, and hope to children facing difficult circumstances.

My humble beginnings continue to guide my purpose. They remind me that even the smallest act of kindness can change someone's life. I believe we are connected not by blood, but by our deeds. Every child deserves to be seen, supported, and given the opportunity to thrive.

"I may not change the whole world, but I can help change one child's world."
WHY N.E.S.T. EXISTS

Because Potential Should Never Be Limited By Circumstance.

Behind every diagnosis is a child. Behind every difficult circumstance is a dream waiting to be nurtured. Behind every learner who struggles is the possibility of success—if only someone is willing to walk the journey with them.

N.E.S.T. exists because I refuse to believe that disability, poverty, abandonment, or disadvantage should decide how far a child can go.

Using my training in Special Education, I create individualized learning experiences, encourage families, advocate for inclusion, celebrate every milestone, and remind every child that their story is still being written.

This mission is not built on having everything. It is built on using what I have, where I am, to make sure more children are seen, supported, and given the opportunity to thrive.

HOPE IN ACTION

These Are Not Statistics.
These Are Children I Have Come To Know.

Every weekend I meet children whose stories are rarely told. Some have lost one parent. Some have lost both. Some are being raised by grandparents doing everything they can with very little. Some are living with disabilities or learning differences that make school more challenging. None of them should ever be defined by those circumstances. These are not success stories. They are simply moments I have witnessed. Moments that remind me why I keep coming back.

⚽

Barasa Stepped Into The Circle

When I first met Barasa, he preferred standing several metres away while the other children played. I never asked him to join us. I greeted him every time I visited, spoke to him whenever I had the opportunity, and allowed him to participate in his own time. One afternoon I rolled a football towards him. He picked it up. Looked at me. Rolled it back. A few minutes later he quietly walked into the circle and remained there until the game ended. Nobody celebrated. But I knew something important had just happened.

"Sometimes belonging begins with one small step."
📚

Naliaka Read Her First Paragraph

Every time books came out, Naliaka became quiet. She avoided eye contact because she was afraid I would ask her to read aloud. Instead of asking her to read a page, we started with one word. Then another. Then one sentence. A few weeks later she confidently read an entire paragraph. The smile afterwards was worth far more than perfect pronunciation.

"Confidence grows one word at a time."
🧩

Wekesa Built His Own Lesson

Wekesa found it difficult to sit still for long periods. Rather than forcing him into one way of learning, I changed my approach. We counted bottle tops. We sorted leaves. We built shapes using cartons and sticks. Without realising it, he was learning mathematics, language and problem-solving through play. That afternoon reminded me that children don't all learn the same way.

"Sometimes the lesson simply needs another door."
🎨

Nasimiyu Finally Laughed

During my first visits, Nasimiyu hardly spoke. She stayed close to the older girls and avoided attention. I never pressured her to interact. I simply kept showing up. One afternoon during an art activity she laughed. Later she asked if she could help me hand out crayons to the younger children. It reminded me that trust cannot be hurried.

"Trust grows through consistency."
🌧️

Some Days Are Hard

Not every visit ends with smiles. Some children arrive hungry. Some are grieving. Some don't want to participate. Some days I leave wishing I could do far more than I was able to do. Those days matter too. They remind me that real change doesn't happen in a single afternoon. It happens because someone keeps returning.

"Hope returns when someone keeps coming back."

Why I Keep Coming Back

People often ask why I spend my weekends doing this. My answer has never changed. Every child deserves one adult who notices them. One person who celebrates the smallest victories. One person who believes in their future before they believe in it themselves. That is what N.E.S.T. means to me. One teacher. One mission. Walking alongside children who deserve every opportunity to thrive.

MY STORY

One Teacher.
One Mission.

My name is Catherine Lukale, and I am a Special Education teacher in Kenya.

My story is one of hope, resilience, and the life-changing impact of kindness. I was raised in a humble background where pursuing an education often seemed beyond my reach. Someone chose to believe in me, held my hand, and gave me the opportunity to continue my education. That single act of generosity changed the course of my life forever.

Because someone invested in my future, I chose to invest my life in the futures of others. My passion led me into Special Needs Education, where I have the privilege of supporting children with diverse abilities, helping them learn, grow, and discover the confidence to reach their full potential.

Beyond the classroom, my heart is devoted to orphaned and vulnerable children. I strive to be the helping hand that I once needed, offering encouragement, care, and hope to children facing difficult circumstances.

My humble beginnings continue to guide my purpose. They remind me that even the smallest act of kindness can change someone's life. I believe we are connected not by blood, but by our deeds. Every child deserves to be seen, supported, and given the opportunity to thrive.

"I may not change the whole world, but I can help change one child's world."
WHY N.E.S.T. EXISTS

Because Potential Should Never Be Limited By Circumstance.

Behind every diagnosis is a child. Behind every difficult circumstance is a dream waiting to be nurtured. Behind every learner who struggles is the possibility of success—if only someone is willing to walk the journey with them.

N.E.S.T. exists because I refuse to believe that disability, poverty, abandonment, or disadvantage should decide how far a child can go.

Using my training in Special Education, I create individualized learning experiences, encourage families, advocate for inclusion, celebrate every milestone, and remind every child that their story is still being written.

This mission is not built on having everything. It is built on using what I have, where I am, to make sure more children are seen, supported, and given the opportunity to thrive.

LEARNING TOGETHER

Every Child Has Been One Of My Greatest Teachers.

Every weekend, every classroom, every home visit, and every conversation has taught me something new. My work is not about seeing disabilities. It is about seeing children, understanding how they learn, and finding ways to help them thrive. Over time I have learned that small changes in how we teach, encourage, and include a child can make an extraordinary difference. These are some of the lessons the children themselves have taught me.

👁️

Visual Impairment

One thing I learned very quickly is that children with visual impairment do not need pity. They need opportunities to use the abilities they already possess.

I discovered that clear instructions, patience, tactile learning materials, and allowing children to explore independently often build confidence much faster than constantly doing everything for them.

What parents often discover: Independence grows when children are trusted, encouraged, and allowed to participate.
🧩

Autism

Working with autistic learners has taught me that behaviour is often a form of communication. Every child wants to connect, even if they express themselves differently.

I have learned that routines, predictability, patience, and understanding a child's interests create an environment where learning becomes less overwhelming and confidence begins to grow.

One lesson I carry with me: Progress is not measured by how much a child behaves like everyone else, but by how confidently they become themselves.
♿

Cerebral Palsy

Cerebral palsy has reminded me that physical limitations do not define intelligence, dreams, or potential.

Sometimes learning simply requires more time, adapted materials, or a different way of participating. When barriers are removed, children often surprise everyone around them.

What I have seen: Inclusion begins when we stop focusing on what a child cannot do and start building on what they can.
🌻

Down Syndrome

Learners with Down syndrome have shown me that consistent encouragement, repetition, and genuine celebration of small achievements build remarkable confidence.

Every new word spoken, every new skill learned, and every independent task completed deserves to be recognised because those moments become the foundation for future growth.

Something every family should know: High expectations, combined with patient support, help children discover abilities they may never have realised they possessed.
🧠

Intellectual Disability

Every learner develops differently. Success is not always measured by grades but by growth, independence, confidence, and participation.

Breaking learning into smaller steps, celebrating progress, and creating opportunities for success has shown me that every child can continue learning when given the right support.

What matters most: Never compare a child's journey with someone else's. Growth looks different for every learner.
❤️

Orphaned and Vulnerable Children

One of the greatest lessons I have learned is that every child longs to feel safe, valued, and noticed. Before learning can flourish, trust often has to be rebuilt.

Sometimes the greatest difference is made by consistently showing up, listening without judgement, and reminding a child that their future is not determined by their circumstances.

I have learned this: Hope often begins with one caring adult who chooses not to give up on a child.

HOPE IN ACTION

Every Child Has A Story.

Behind every smile is a journey of courage, resilience, and hope.

Every weekend I meet children whose stories are rarely told. Some have lost one parent. Some have lost both. Some are being raised by grandparents doing everything they can with very little. Some are living with disabilities or learning differences that make school more challenging. None of them should ever be defined by those circumstances. These are not success stories. They are simply moments I have witnessed. Moments that remind me why I keep coming back.

⚽

Barasa Stepped Into The Circle

Barasa always watched from a distance. One afternoon he quietly joined a football game. Nobody applauded. But that small step marked the beginning of belonging.

"Sometimes belonging begins with one small step."
📚

Naliaka Read Her First Paragraph

Every time books came out, Naliaka became quiet. She avoided eye contact because she was afraid I would ask her to read aloud. Instead of asking her to read a page, we started with one word. Then another. Then one sentence. A few weeks later she confidently read an entire paragraph. The smile afterwards was worth far more than perfect pronunciation.

"Confidence grows one word at a time."
🧩

Wekesa Built His Own Lesson

Wekesa found it difficult to sit still for long periods. Rather than forcing him into one way of learning, I changed my approach. We counted bottle tops. We sorted leaves. We built shapes using cartons and sticks. Without realising it, he was learning mathematics, language and problem-solving through play. That afternoon reminded me that children don't all learn the same way.

"Sometimes the lesson simply needs another door."
🎨

Nasimiyu Finally Laughed

During my first visits, Nasimiyu hardly spoke. She stayed close to the older girls and avoided attention. I never pressured her to interact. I simply kept showing up. One afternoon during an art activity she laughed. Later she asked if she could help me hand out crayons to the younger children. It reminded me that trust cannot be hurried.

"Trust grows through consistency."
🌧️

Some Days Are Hard

Not every visit ends with smiles. Some children arrive hungry. Some are grieving. Some don't want to participate. Some days I leave wishing I could do far more than I was able to do. Those days matter too. They remind me that real change doesn't happen in a single afternoon. It happens because someone keeps returning.

"Hope returns when someone keeps coming back."

Why I Keep Coming Back

People often ask why I spend my weekends doing this. My answer has never changed. Every child deserves one adult who notices them. One person who celebrates the smallest victories. One person who believes in their future before they believe in it themselves. That is what N.E.S.T. means to me. One teacher. One mission. Walking alongside children who deserve every opportunity to thrive.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Questions I Am Often Asked

Can children with disabilities really learn alongside other children?

Yes. With the right support, patience, and appropriate teaching approaches, inclusive classrooms benefit both children with and without disabilities.

My child learns slowly. Does that mean they cannot succeed?

Every child learns differently. Progress may look different, but every child has the capacity to grow when given the right opportunities.

Why do you spend time visiting families at home?

Learning does not begin and end in the classroom. Understanding a child's home environment helps me support both the learner and the family more effectively.

Is disability caused by a curse or punishment?

No. Research and lived experience consistently show that disabilities have medical, genetic, developmental, or environmental causes. Every child deserves dignity, respect, and opportunity.

What is the biggest lesson you have learned?

Children do not need perfect circumstances to flourish. They need adults who believe in them, remain patient, and refuse to give up.
